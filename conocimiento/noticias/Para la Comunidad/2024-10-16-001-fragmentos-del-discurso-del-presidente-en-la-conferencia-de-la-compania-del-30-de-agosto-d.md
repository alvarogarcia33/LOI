## Contenido

Today we have many important topics to consider, and I would like to start with the evolution of our business. 

 We constantly talk about the fact that it is impossible to grow beautiful fruits if you do not care for and do not remember about the soil and roots. It is impossible to grow a harvest without experience, knowledge and tools, and most importantly — without the right attitude and approach. 

 And we are not only proud of our achievements, which we have achieved thanks to your trust, your persistence and your hard work, but we understand with absolute clarity that these are the very roots with which we can achieve absolutely incredible results in the future. 

 As we know, in any business there is a boundary beyond which it is almost impossible to go. Typically, all restrictions in a business are related to its product or the geography of its use. 

 That is why business especially values solutions that allow companies to go beyond these types of restrictions. 

 But let me, before I move on to future events, put all this in the context of our history, that is, take a brief excursion into our past. 

 Those who attended our conferences in 2020 remember that we then presented a five-year plan for our development not as an individual business in itself, but as an entire ecosystem of various business solutions. 

 For those who have not yet been to our conferences, I will allow myself a brief retelling of these ideas, since this is most directly related to the main topic of the conference. 

 So, the central idea of our business plan five years ago was an approach that could be described as transforming the business from a product to a service. 

 I repeat! The central idea of our business plan five years ago was an approach that could be described as transforming the business from a product to a service. 
   
 The “Product — Service” scale 

 If we look at business as an activity, then we can say, with some reservations, that business — regardless of time and economic structure — is about creating value for clients. 

 The whole point is that at different times and under different economic conditions, different values need to be created for clients. 

 One hundred and fifty years ago you could create value by producing horse-drawn carriages; one hundred years ago you could create new value by mass, assembly line production of inexpensive cars; fifty years ago you could create value by developing television; thirty years ago by developing Internet connections and mobile phones; twenty years ago — online distribution of music and video. Fifteen years ago smartphones appeared, etc. 

 The world has changed, business and the rules of the game have changed. 

 The “new normal” in business is now different, and entrepreneurs are now often forced to focus on creating new value for the simple reason that the “lifespan” of business models is significantly reduced. 

 I often asked myself and my entire team the following questions: 
 1. What new value will be in demand after the next crisis? 
 2. What skills do Leaders need to have to lead businesses through these changes? 

 This is a very interesting question, very interesting... 
 I think it is clear to all of you why these questions are so important for the company and for you. 

 Obviously, we want not only to provide our clients with modern products and services today, but also to have progressive products and services in the future. 

 And, besides, we need to expand the groups of potential consumers and like-minded people. 
 These are vital questions, and the right answers to them will ensure our future. 

 In all this evolution of value, an attentive observer will be able to notice two important trends: 
 the first trend is that the quality of products themselves that were produced and put on the market became higher, they became more technologically advanced and cheaper; 
 the second trend is that along with the evolution of the product, there was a change in the LOGIC of the business: the logic of the product was replaced by the logic of the service. In other words, over the past ten years, the service component has taken dominant positions in almost all businesses. 

At one of the conferences in 2020, I gave the example of Apple, which very clearly demonstrates this idea. 

 Apple combines the sale of very attractive physical products (players, smartphones, computers, etc.) with the digital services of the online store, namely the iTunes Store — this is what makes the company’s products extremely successful. 

 As of April 2020, iTunes Store offered 60 million songs, 25,000 TV shows and 65,000 movies. The store had 575 million active user accounts and serves more than 315 million mobile devices. 

In the third column we see the term “marginality”. To avoid misunderstanding, I will explain this concept to you. 

 Marginality is an indicator of the profitability of a product. That is, marginality shows us how profitable or unprofitable it is to sell a particular product. There are high-margin and low-margin products. All other things being equal, the former bring more profit with the same investment. 

 From this table we can draw two key conclusions: 
 1. Marginality, that is, the profitability of Apple service products is almost twice as high as that of hardware products. 
 2. The main thing: would Apple gadgets be so in demand without service products? 
 Think about these factors. 

 Apple’s service business is managed by two stores: the App Store and Apple Music. 
 In May 2019, Piper Jaffray’s analyst Michael Olson stated that Apple had grown its service business so much that at that moment it was worth $502 billion. 

 At the same time, Olson estimated that Apple’s hardware business was worth less — only $398.8 billion. 

 This means that Apple’s service business was more valuable than its hardware business.   

 Today, Apple’s total valuation is already more than $2 trillion and, as you may have guessed, the main driver of growth is, of course, the service area. 

 The ecosystem around Apple services continues to grow thanks to integration across platforms, which allows seamless management of music, movies, and devices. 

 As of 2024, Apple TV+ has approximately 25 million paid subscribers. In addition, about 50 million users have free access through various promotions, such as a free year with the purchase of an Apple device. This brings the total number of active users to more than 75 million worldwide. 

 If we move away from this, albeit a very clear example of Apple, then, in general, we could look at two opposing logics in business: 
 1. Absolute product logic. 
 2. Absolute service logic. 

 What is an absolute product? This is a theoretical product, completely material, which does not contain any service — only the product itself in its pure form. 

 I use the term “theoretical” because such “pure” products do not exist in nature, because any product contains at least a small part of service, for example, the service for selling this product. 

 What is an absolute service? This is such a theoretical service, completely intangible, which does not contain any physical product. 

 Again, I use the term “theoretical” because such services do not exist in nature. In any service there is always at least a small part of the product that would be used during the provision of this service. For example, the premises where this service would be provided to clients. 

 To demonstrate this to you more clearly, I will show you the following picture: 

This picture marks the “absolute” products, whatever they are, at the top. And below are the “absolute” services, whatever they are. 

 And then we can place any product, any business between these two extremes. It’s like in life, you can place any of your attitudes towards something between “love” and “hate”. 

 Let’s take sugar, for example: it is located not far from the “absolute” product. This means that there is little service in the bag of sugar yet: the sugar has not yet been weighed, it has not been packaged in packs and distributed to the stores where end customers usually buy it. 

 Among other things, this also means that there’s next to not difference between one bag of sugar and another bag of sugar and these two bags of sugar are the same, and therefore easily interchangeable with each other. 

 One ton of cotton can just as easily be interchanged with another, one barrel of oil with another, one bar of gold with another bar of the same fineness. 

 In contrast to this state of affairs, let’s look at the other end of the line — an absolute service. For example, let’s look at the hair salon business. 

 Two hairdressers who work in the same hairdressing salon very often do the same haircuts to the same clients in different ways — that is, they provide different services. 

 And that is why, oftentimes, when we go to a hairdresser, we go to “our” hairdresser that we pick. We do not consider that two services from two different hairdressers in the same salon to be the same and easily interchangeable. 

 The closest thing to our example with a hairdresser, at the second end of the line of “absolute” service — we can put consulting as an example. If you ask two different people for advice, you can often get two different pieces of advice and two different recommendations for action. In addition, consulting is a service in which the material part is usually small, such as a pen and a piece of paper, but the intangible part, in the form of knowledge and experience, can be very large. 

 In the middle, between these two points — an “absolute” product and an “absolute” service — is the restaurant business. This business is approximately fifty percent physical products (meat, fish, vegetables, etc.) and fifty percent service (waiter service, friendly atmosphere, cook service, etc.). 

 So we can evaluate any business in terms of what it has more of: tangible products or intangible services. 

 The general trend of the last twenty years is that customer needs are not limited to the sole need to own a product. After all, if that were the case, there wouldn’t be so many restaurants, cafes, repair shops and hairdressers. 

 The main idea in modern business is not to sell products and services separately, but to sell a specific result from using these products. 

 For example, when you go to a restaurant, you get the result — delicious, properly cooked food in a pleasant atmosphere. This outcome is different from the process of buying groceries in a store, delivering them to your home, cooking them, etc. 

 Finally, when products and services are integrated, it can create new features that would not be available without such integration. For example, iPhone will look less attractive without Apple Store or iTunes Store than with them. 

 In the most general sense, this is exactly why we need the metaverse — the tool through which we can integrate virtually limitless number of functions into any product. 

 We have looked at the “Product — Service” scale. 
 The main thing that is important for us as a company and for you is the global trend in the development of any business, including ours. 

 And the vector of this trend is aimed at increasing the share of services in global sales. Even when selling the simplest product, to maintain competitiveness, it is necessary to increasingly package this product into elements of services. 

 And this is nothing but a business revolution that has already happened right before our very eyes. 

 From the standpoint of long-term survival, today it is no longer enough to sell “just” a product. 

 Where would Microsoft be now if this company had stopped only at creating an operating system without the idea of creating related service products and services, without creating an ecosystem that allows third-party developers to create their own products based on it? 

 And note that Microsoft does not stop there, it evolves: it buys Skype, Minecraft, LinkedIn, etc. 

 The result of such evolution in the field of business development is obvious: 
 
- Microsoft’s turnover in the first year of operation was $16,005; 

- Microsoft’s turnover in 2019 was $125.8 billion. 
 The growth is obvious, and it is impressive! 

 Here I emphasize an important point: all modern successful business models are result-oriented, not product-oriented! 

 It is this category of business models that is closest to an absolute service. 

 The key point here is that in order for the consumer to get the “absolute service,” he or she no longer needs to own any goods, tools, or resources. This factor has fundamentally changed the entire business landscape over the last decade. 

 For example, to move from one city to another city by car, a UBER client does not need to own a car, buy insurance, obtain a driver’s license, etc. All you need for this trip is your phone: download the app and press the button. 

 Ready! The result is achieved — a car with a driver has come to pick you up and now you are on your way to another city. And this is exactly the product in the form of service that was of interest to the buyer. 

 All this brings us to an understanding of the next evolutionary step in our development. 

 With this, I want to end my presentation of the historical context in which our previous development plan was born, which I announced in 2020 and which we successfully implemented in the form of what we have today. 
 Now we are ready to take the next step forward. 

 And what I want to say now is: 

 “PRODUCT — SERVICE — EXPERIENCE” 

 Yes, you are not mistaken, the new business logic of the next decade is based on EXPERIENCES. 

 This new logic has its own tool with which we can achieve new experiences for our clients. And, as many of you may have already guessed, I am now talking about a new component in any successful business model of the future — GAMIFICATION. 

 We talked about this at our conference in July, and let me just quickly refresh this information. Take Waze, for example. 

 Waze turned navigation into an exciting adventure. 

 Imagine that your daily commute turns into an exciting adventure where you are the main character, battling traffic jams and unexpected road hazards. Welcome to the world of Waze, the app that has completely changed the navigation game rules. Forget boring maps and monotonous voice instructions — Waze makes every ride fun and interactive thanks to gamification. 

 Now let’s look at this in the context of a relatively new trend in business models. 

 In modern business, gamification is becoming a powerful tool that helps companies increase the engagement, loyalty and productivity of their clients and employees. 

 This approach, originally applied in the gaming sector, is increasingly being used in a variety of industries, from education to healthcare to finance, and is increasingly becoming a key element in many business models. 

 Personalization, education, social interactions, health, finance, B2B and sustainability are just some of the key areas where gamification is demonstrating its effectiveness. 

 By following these global trends, companies can not only improve their business models, but also create more engaging and meaningful experiences for their customers and employees. 

 As I said before, gamification can be seen as the next stage in the evolution of business, moving from product to service and then to engaging and interacting with clients on a deeper level. 

 Gamification turns spectators into active participants. 

 The system of rewards, levels and challenges keeps their attention and motivates them to return. Instead of a monotonous shopping or service use, clients embark on an exciting adventure. 

 Instead of boring corporate trainings there are game simulators. Instead of faceless KPIs — bright badges and levels. Gamification makes work more fun and productive. Employees become involved, motivated and strive for new achievements. 

 Gamification creates communities. 

 People unite, compete, share successes and support each other. This turns an ordinary app or service into a living ecosystem where everyone feels important. 

 Game elements stimulate creativity and innovation. Companies that implement gamification are one step ahead of the competition by offering unique and immersive experiences. 

 Gamification is not just a trend, it is the future of business modeling. It makes brand interactions fun, work — interesting, and companies — successful. 

 It is very important to understand that the key to gamification is the metaverse. 
 The metaverse is an ideal tool with which you can implement almost any game mechanics — from the most primitive to the most complex. 

 Another important factor related to the metaverse will be that the metaverse creates its own economy with virtual currency. 
 This not only motivates active participation, but also makes every action in the metaverse financially significant. 

 We have already talked about all this one way or another, and I just wanted to make sure that we are all on the “same page” if we talk about this from the point of view of the history of the development of our company. 

 The key point here is that gamification is one of the strongest trends today, and the metaverse is a tool that allows you to bring all gamification ideas to life, much like you can use a hammer to drive a nail into the wall and hang a picture. 

In the world of the future, those who make life a game win!
